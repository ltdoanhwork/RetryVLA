## 2. Positive evidence: what the LRS-trained policies actually do wrong
- **Gripper chatter at val.** Mean gripper sign flips per episode: lrs2 s7 / s8 / s9 = 13.6 / 12.4 / 12.2, v1 s8
  17.9, v2-GT 3.9, against Original s10 / s11 = 3.2 / 2.6, C0 3.0 and G2-phys 2.2. Share of episodes with >= 10
  flips: 0.35-0.52 vs 0.07-0.08. Failed lrs2_s7 episodes average 21.6 flips and run the full 300 steps. Event
  detectors fire on only 7-8 % of lrs2 episodes, against 60 % failures: the policy opens and closes on hover, it
  does not knock objects over.
- **The teacher windows teach it.** Share of 50-step target chunks that contain >= 2 gripper toggles (close and
  reopen): D0 2.7 %, v1 12 %, v2 34 %, v2-GT 28 %. Two long q = 1.0 windows (turn_on_stove 300 frames with 12 flips;
  wine-cabinet 277 frames with 13 flips) are 4 of 5 judge false positives, and 32 % of v2 frames are static.
- **Per-dim FM loss at M0** (`runs/_analysis/lrs_debug/m0_loss.json`, 4 noise draws): D0 0.129 (gripper 0.048);
  v2 windows 1.12 = 8.7x. The gripper dim is 1.58 = **33x D0**, x 1.78, z 1.58, rot-y 1.17. Five episodes (7, 1,
  0, 17, 4) carry 82 % of the v2 window loss. At a 4.1 % anchor share, the windows therefore carry ~26 % of the
  gradient, and its gripper part is about as large as D0's entire gripper gradient.
- **v2-GT (sim-verified windows only, 655 anchors) = 77**, inside the Original / C0 range, with flips 3.9. The damage
  is concentrated in the judge-accepted false-positive windows.

Working hypothesis (not yet confirmed by C1 / C3): the remaining drop is a data effect, not a bug. Unpredictable,
high-loss teacher actions are mostly gripper toggles while hovering, and they come from judge false positives
(precision 0.07). Through closed-loop resampling every 5 steps, they become a per-replan chatter probability that
compounds over 60 replans. Caveat: the 82 -> 35 effect size is still larger than this mechanism predicts on its own,
so C1 / C3 must still rule out a bug in the recovery-root code path.
