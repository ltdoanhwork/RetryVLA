## 2. Problem

A frozen VLA (pi0.5, RDT, XR-0) fails under OOD disturbances: the target is **occluded** from the head camera, or the
object **slips** away (displaced 4 cm at step 40). Monitor-then-repair pipelines first detect a failure and then
trigger a scripted repair. But a failure signal does not say whether a repair would beat simply continuing, or which
repair to use.

## 3. Core idea: what it fundamentally exploits

At every action-chunk boundary, ForkCast compares the **counterfactual futures** of a few candidates from the *same*
state: **Commit** (the policy's own nominal chunk), **Reobserve** (move the head camera, then re-query the policy) and
**Replan** (motion-planner re-grasp at the perceived object position). A V-JEPA 2-AC latent WM predicts each
candidate's head-camera latent at a common horizon (64 steps). A shared progress head scores it, and the argmax
candidate is executed.

What it fundamentally exploits:
1. **Within-state relative ranking.** Only candidates from the same state are compared, so scores need not be
   calibrated across states or tasks. Because Commit is always in the set, "whether to intervene" and "how" become one
   argmax: no failure detector and no threshold.
2. **Repairs that lie outside the policy's action distribution.** These are a movable camera and a perception-driven
   motion planner (OWLv2 + SAM + Depth Anything V2). They add capabilities the VLA does not have, and the WM only has
   to choose among about 3 options.
3. **A common horizon** that makes candidates of different duration comparable.

## 4. Method

| Aspect | ForkCast |
|---|---|
| WM type | latent, **V-JEPA 2-AC** (frozen ViT-g encoder; predictor initialised from V-JEPA 2-AC, last 4 blocks trained). Conditioned on 3 channels: per-step EE increments, cumulative EE displacement and camera offset. Predicts M = 8 milestones autoregressively, h = 8 steps each (H_f = 64), from 16 history frames |
| WM use | **test-time** candidate scoring at every decision point (H_a = 32; first decision at step 64). Latency 1.44 s per decision for 3 serial candidates (RTX 6000 Ada), excluding camera probing and planning |
| Failure source | **injected disturbances**: an occluding pillar on the line of sight, or a 4 cm slip at step 40. Clean episodes too. Not the policy's natural failures |
| Candidates | Commit, Reobserve (21 to 35 discrete camera poses, shortlisted by target-pixel count and off-centre cost), Replan (collision-free planner to perceived centroid + TCP offset, then close the gripper and hand control back). Probing precedes every decision |
| Selection / critic | progress head (mean-pooled tokens, LayerNorm, 2-layer MLP) on the predicted **endpoint** latent. Label **rho = clip(1 - d / d_ref, 0, 1)**, where d = object-to-goal distance (EE-to-bell for Click bell) from sim GT. This is a **geometric task-progress proxy, not P(success) and not policy-conditioned** |
| WM / critic training data | "**mining**": from a decision state, *every* candidate is executed open-loop for H_f steps in sim (branching). Loss = smooth-L1 on latents + progress, with endpoint-weighted milestones. Real robot: 360 branches (30 starts x 4 settings x 3 candidates) with manual resets |
| Verification | **none at deployment.** The "oracle" executes every candidate from the same sim state and picks the highest *realised progress at t + H_f*. It is a privileged upper bound on selection under the proxy, not a task-success check |
| What is trained | WM predictor (partially) + conditioning encoders + progress head. **The VLA is never updated** (LoRA fine-tune on 50 demos per task, then frozen) |
| Training strategy (anti-failure) | not applicable (no policy learning) |

## 5. Benchmarks and key numbers

RoboTwin 2.0 (clean config, no domain randomisation), 5 tasks. 6 episodes per task and condition, so 30 per condition
and 60 pooled-OOD. One training run.

| Method (pi0.5) | clean | slip | occ | pooled-OOD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commit (frozen policy) | 53.3 | 10.0 | 16.7 | 13.3 |
| Fixed Replan | 70.0 | 33.3 | 10.0 | 21.7 |
| Fixed Reobserve | 36.7 | 10.0 | 23.3 | 16.7 |
| Random selection | 53.3 | 16.7 | 16.7 | 16.7 |
| Direct progress regressor (no WM) | 56.7 | 23.3 | 16.7 | 20.0 |
| ForkCast | **76.7** | **33.3** | **26.7** | **30.0** [19.9, 42.5] |
| Oracle (privileged) | 83.3 | 36.7 | 26.7 | 31.7 |

- **Transfer** of the pi0.5-trained selector to other backbones without updates (pooled-OOD): XR-0 0.0 -> 21.7,
  RDT 5.0 -> 23.3. These bases are very weak (clean 16.7 and 10.0).
- **Real robot** (AgileX PiPER, 30 trials per setting, bar labels of Fig. 5): clean sponge 73.3 -> 86.7, sponge in
  box 16.7 -> 50.0, can insertion 40.0 -> 66.7, can behind lid 6.7 -> 33.3.
- **Selection diagnostics** (Tab. III c): on the 16 of 30 candidate sets where outcomes differ, best-set hit rate is
  75 % (Random 37.5 %, Direct 25 %). WM-fidelity checks are weak: latent-effect cosine <= 0.03, within-state Spearman
  0.16-0.27, and an independently retrained replica gets 0.16.

## 6. Limitations (theirs and ours)

- Self-stated: bounded by the predefined candidates and **adds no new skills to the policy**; ~1.4 s per decision plus
  camera motion (a 10 cm lift takes ~5 s on the real robot).
- Statistics: 60 pooled episodes. ForkCast's CI [19.9, 42.5] overlaps fixed Replan [13.1, 33.6] and Direct. The gain
  over the best fixed strategy is +5 successes out of 60. No seeds, no paired test.
- **Most of the clean gain comes from the Replan primitive** (fixed Replan already gives 70.0 on clean, vs 76.7 for
  ForkCast). The oracle is only +1.7 above ForkCast, so selection is near its ceiling: the bottleneck is the
  candidate set, not the WM.
- Search pressure is minimal (2-3 candidates). This is the regime where WM optimism is small (see section 8).
- The progress label needs privileged geometry (sim state, or real-robot segmentation + depth) and is task-specific.
  It scores a 64-step endpoint and ignores whether the policy can finish *after* the horizon.
- Disturbances are synthetic and single-type per episode. Reobserve needs an actuated head camera, which is
  unavailable in LIBERO's fixed views.

## 8. Honest overlap verdict

**Overlap: low to moderate.** It sits in the test-time steering cluster (with DreamAvoid, DreamSteer, VeriVLA, Back on
Track's planner), not the data-generation cluster. It is less close than Back on Track 2610.19579 (policy-conditioned
value plus a WM planner), 2610.05151 (continuation acceptance plus distill) or SAILOR (search plus distill).

Pre-empted (cannot be claimed, mostly already conceded in `WM_RECOVERY_OVERLAP.md` section 3):
- "Use an action-conditioned latent WM (incl. V-JEPA 2-AC) to rank recovery candidates against the policy's own
  continuation".
- "Hand-designed repair primitives as candidates beat native policy samples". Their fixed Replan alone is +8 pt
  pooled; this also agrees with our M11 and with 2610.37196.
- "Train the WM / scorer on branched executions from the same state".
- "Detector-free recovery decision" at test time.

Not pre-empted (still ours):
1. **Natural failures -> recovery training data**: converting the policy's own failures into verified data and
   **distilling into the VLA**, with no test-time module. ForkCast itself says it "does not add new manipulation
   skills to the frozen policy". That limitation is our Phase 2 motivation, in their words.
2. **C^pi vs progress**: ForkCast's scorer is a geometric progress proxy at a fixed horizon. Our matched comparison
   (C^pi solved 0.48 vs goal 0.24 / progress 0.21; SFT RSR 0.42-0.43 vs 0.19 / 0.18) is direct evidence against that
   design for recovery data. Caveat: our generic success critic B4 is as good as C^pi, so claim "outcome-labelled
   continuation critic vs progress", not "policy-specific".
3. **GT verification by the policy's continuation, under search pressure**, plus the **optimism measurement**. With
   N = 3, ForkCast sits near its oracle (+1.7 pt gap). Our own data say why: the same V-JEPA 2-AC has FP ~0.70 at
   N = 8 but ~0.83-0.85 at N = 128-512 (RESULTS "RQ3 follow-up", exploitation curve). So low-N test-time selection is
   safe, and large-N search, which we need to *find* recoveries for hard natural failures, is not without
   verification. This turns ForkCast into supporting evidence for RQ3 rather than a competitor.
4. Anti-failure training recipe (bridge-only, share, nominal replay) and the LIBERO-Goal mix-share diagnosis: absent.
5. First method numbers on LIBERO-Recover: still true.

## 9. Recommendations

**Repositioning (one sentence for intro / related work).** "Test-time recovery selectors (ForkCast, DreamAvoid,
DreamSteer, Back on Track) keep the VLA frozen and pay a WM + perception cost at every decision. They are bounded by a
handful of candidates and add no skills to the policy. RetryVLA instead *searches* many candidates offline from the
policy's own failures, accepts only bridges after which the policy actually finishes (ground truth), and amortises
them into the policy." Stress that search pressure is the regime where WM selection breaks (our FP vs N curve). That
is exactly why verification is needed for data generation, while low-N selection like ForkCast gets away without it.

**Main table?** Not as a faithful reproduction. No code is out, RoboTwin is a different benchmark, and Reobserve needs
an actuated camera (LIBERO agent / wrist views are fixed and SmolVLA was trained on them). Instead, add a
**"ForkCast-style test-time selector"** row to the *test-time block* of the main table (next to the Back-on-Track arm
and the existing online best-of-8), because reviewers will see it as the cheapest alternative:
- candidates per chunk boundary = {Commit (the pi chunk), 2-4 library primitives incl. a re-grasp toward the object;
  use the observation-only geometry-head pose, with the GT pose as an upper bound only};
- WM = our V-JEPA 2-AC adapter (their WM) and WM v1; common horizon = bridge length (padded); argmax, Commit wins ties;
- scorer = **progress head** on the predicted latent (reuse the B3-progress value; label = object-to-goal distance
  from BDDL predicates), as in the paper.
- Run on pi0 and pi1, Spatial test RSR (560 eps) + nominal + latency.
- Effort: ~1-1.5 days engineering (reuse the online best-of-8 loop in `retryvla/eval`, candidate generator from
  `retryvla/search/primitives.py`, scorers from `retryvla/search/scorers.py`). Eval: ~4 arms x 560 eps; estimate
  ~10-20 GPU-h at best-of-8-like cost (WM calls dominate; V-JEPA is ~200x slower per candidate than WM v1, so cap
  V-JEPA at 1 arm). Queue after the Back-on-Track arm and F1 (shell 953bb534); coordinate with that agent so the two
  test-time arms share code.

**Ablations that separate us (pre-register in PLAN before running):**
1. **Scorer swap at test time**: the ForkCast-style selector with progress head vs with C^pi, same candidates.
   This shows our critic result is not specific to data generation.
2. **Search pressure**: the same selector with N = 3 (ForkCast regime) vs N = 32 / 512 (our regime), reporting
   realised success, FP and the imagined-vs-real gap. Expect near-oracle at N = 3 and a growing optimism gap with N.
   This supports "verification is required for search-based data generation" with their WM.
3. **Distill vs select at matched compute**: pi0 + ForkCast-style vs pi1 (distilled, no test-time module) vs
   pi1 + ForkCast-style. We already know pi0 + best-of-8 over *VLA samples* = 0.118 (no gain); with primitives as
   candidates, the test-time arm is a fairer test. Report latency (theirs ~1.4 s per decision).
4. Optional: a "Commit-in-set" detector-free test-time arm on nominal LIBERO, to show whether always-on selection
   hurts nominal success (ForkCast reports a clean gain, but driven by Replan).
